Hiryu, the WMS and the TMS — who does what

The target design for the build and business teams. It fixes the boundary between orders, the warehouse floor and delivery, so each job is built once, in one place.

Every order enters through Hiryu. Only Hiryu talks to the WMS. Only the WMS counts the shelf.

3Stock: one counter, two numbers

The WMS counts the shelf. Hiryu works out what each platform may sell from that count. Hiryu never keeps a count of its own.

Hiryu never deducts a completed sale — the WMS number already includes it. The only thing Hiryu subtracts itself is orders in transit, normally for about a second. If the WMS is down, that hold grows and stops Hiryu selling from a stale number.

How Hiryu fans one number out to many listings

  1. Take the WMS's available for that SKU at that dark store.
  2. Subtract orders in transit.
  3. Apply the split rule. Our own channels read the full number live at checkout. Grab gets it pushed, minus a buffer when stock is low.
  4. For bundles, divide by the quantity in "Sold as" and round down. A bundle of 4 shows ⌊available ÷ 4⌋.
  5. Push only the listings whose number changed — absolute numbers, through a retrying queue.

Why the buffer is on Grab only. Every order passes through Hiryu, so two orders for the last unit are handled one at a time and the second fails at intake. On our own channels that means "sold out" at checkout. On Grab the customer has already paid, so it means a cancellation, which hurts the store's rating — and the Malaysia contract lets Grab deactivate a station on 7 days' notice. The buffer is set per SKU, defaults to zero, and turns on below a threshold.

6Routing and the last mile

Which dark store — own channels only

  • Nearest dark store that can fill the whole order within the promise.
  • No split orders. Two riders on one small basket costs more than it earns.
  • If none can fill it whole, offer what's in stock, or next-day.
  • Grab orders arrive already routed — Grab shows customers the stores near them.

Which last mile — proposed defaults, to confirm

  • Grab channel → Grab rider. Grab decides, not us.
  • Inside ~7 km, riders free, promise reachable → Ninja dedicated rider.
  • Riders full, or outside their radius → on-demand third party.
  • Customer accepts slower, or no dark store reaches them → next-day.
Option 1
Grab rider

Grab orders only. Assigned by Grab before the order reaches us.

Option 2
Ninja dedicated rider

The fixed-wage same-day fleet, ~50% utilised today.

Option 3
On-demand third party

GrabExpress, Lalamove — overflow and out-of-radius.

Option 4
Next-day network

The regular network, when speed isn't needed or can't be met.

7What changes

In Hiryu

  • Stops keeping its own stock count. "Stock on hand" becomes a derived sellable number per listing.
  • Gains: orders from non-Grab channels, dark-store routing, the split rule, the last-mile choice, one connector per platform.
  • Keeps: brands, stores (brand × dark store listings), menus, bundles, the Grab integration.

In Baskoro's WMS PRD — to agree with him

  • §13 / §13.2: the adapter sends orders into Hiryu; routing moves to Hiryu, rider booking to the TMS. His direct route stays as a phase-1 shortcut with a date to fold it in.
  • §2.2 and §12.2.6: the POS stops deducting sales; message 3 carries available after every change. Closes his Q6.
  • Ledger: add the stock owner on every unit.

The Malaysia barcode PRD puts racking codes, barcodes and quantities inside the POS. Under this design those belong to the WMS: its feature ideas feed the merge, its placement doesn't.
